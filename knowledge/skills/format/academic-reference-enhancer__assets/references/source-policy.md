# Source Policy

Use reliable, traceable sources for discovery and verification.

## Preferred Academic Sources

- DOI resolver and Crossref
- Publisher pages such as IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, Wiley, Oxford Academic, MDPI, and journal websites
- Indexing and discovery sources such as Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, DBLP, PubMed, arXiv only for preprint status, CNKI, Wanfang, and VIP

## Preferred Official Sources

- National standard platforms
- Government, ministry, regulator, and standards-body websites
- Official project, dataset, vulnerability, and standard registries

For cybersecurity work, prefer official sources such as CVE, NVD, CNVD, CNNVD, CWE, CPE, CVSS, EPSS, CISA KEV, MITRE ATT&CK, and CAPEC for facts about vulnerabilities, taxonomies, metrics, and threat techniques.

## Sources That Need Corroboration

Do not use these as the only evidence for authenticity:

- Blogs, repost sites, content farms, personal pages, and random PDF mirrors
- ResearchGate, Academia.edu, or citation aggregation pages
- Library catalog records without enough metadata to verify the publication
- Search-result snippets without opening the underlying source

## Discovery Output Requirements

When generating candidate references, include:

- Title
- Authors
- Year
- Venue
- Document type
- DOI or official URL when available
- Supported chapter or section
- Supported claim
- Suggested insertion position
- Authenticity status, normally `pending verification`

Do not present discovered candidates as verified until the verification workflow is completed.
