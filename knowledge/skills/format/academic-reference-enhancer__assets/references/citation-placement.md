# Citation Placement

Use citations to support concrete claims, not to decorate paragraphs.

## Placement Rules

- Insert citations at the end of the specific sentence or clause they support.
- If one paragraph contains multiple claims, do not place all references at the paragraph end unless they support a summary statement.
- Use one to three references for a concrete claim.
- Use multiple references only for synthesis, comparison, historical background, or state-of-the-art statements.
- Avoid citation stacks that are longer than necessary.
- Move unsuitable suggested insertion positions and explain why.
- Use citation forms such as `[1]`, `[2-4]`, and `[3,5,7]` according to the citation style and numbering sequence.

## Matching Criteria

Match a reference to a claim only when the source directly supports at least one of:

- Definition, model, method, or taxonomy used in the claim
- Empirical result or benchmark mentioned in the claim
- Standard, policy, regulation, or metric named in the claim
- Prior work, limitation, comparison, or research gap
- Dataset, tool, framework, or system relied on by the document

If the relation is only topical but not claim-supporting, do not cite it for that claim.

## Insertion Plan Fields

For every proposed citation, include:

```text
Chapter:
Section:
Original sentence or keyword:
Proposed citation:
Supported claim:
Corresponding reference:
Reason for insertion:
```
